An expansion roster had little established top-end talent and no competitive continuity. The debut matters enormously to Heat history, but its on-court performance places it last in this ranking.

The season

Miami’s inaugural NBA season began with 17 consecutive losses. Rony Seikaly, Grant Long and Kevin Edwards became early building blocks.

The criteria

Results matter.
Context does, too.

This is an editorial ranking, not an official NBA ranking or a simulated head-to-head tournament. Championships carry the greatest weight. After that, playoff progress, regular-season winning percentage, roster quality, depth, and the team’s demonstrated level of play decide the order. A great playoff run can outweigh a modest regular season; a strong regular season can also outrank a less consistent finalist.

Shortened seasons are judged by winning percentage rather than raw wins. Injuries and roster changes inform the explanation, but hypothetical healthy versions do not replace what actually happened.

Coverage: 1988–89 through 2025–26. The 2026–27 season is excluded because it has not been played. Records are regular-season records; play-in games are reported separately. Series scores show Miami’s wins first.

Rosters: Season-wide lists from RealGM include trades, brief contracts, and some players who did not appear in a game. Positions and jersey numbers follow that archive. Leading scorers are ranked by Miami regular-season points per game, with a minimum of 20 appearances; all averages reflect Miami games only.

Sources: Each season links to its roster and statistics archive and its season history. Rankings and commentary are original editorial assessments. This is a fixed snapshot, researched September 30, 2026.
